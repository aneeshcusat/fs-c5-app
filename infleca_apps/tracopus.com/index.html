The challenge

Disconnected systems create operational blind spots.

When HRMS, project delivery, time capture, payroll, finance, approvals, and executive reporting live in separate tools, every team optimizes locally while the enterprise loses visibility — revenue leaks quietly, payroll exceptions surface at lock, and leaders reconcile spreadsheets instead of running the business.

01

One admin console for every role

Employees, managers, project managers, HR, payroll, finance, and executives are forced into the same navigation and registers — consultants see payroll configuration they should never touch, while CXOs scroll past timesheet grids to find portfolio health. Without persona-aware landing routes, scoped dashboards, and field masking, adoption stalls because every screen feels built for administrators, not for the person doing the work.

02

Payroll blockers discovered too late

Attendance matrices live in one system, leave approvals in another, timesheets in a third, and expense reimbursements somewhere else. Payroll teams only see the gaps at run lock — missing punches, pending leave, unapproved weeks, and reimbursement holds that force emergency corrections, manual overrides, and delayed paychecks. There is no Payroll Readiness Score connecting every prerequisite before processing begins.

03

Revenue leakage between delivery and finance

Billable hours are logged against projects, but invoice drafts are built in a separate finance tool with no traceability to source rows. Approved time sits unbilled; purchase orders expire without burn alerts; margin erodes on fixed-fee engagements because est-vs-actual never rolls up to billing readiness. Month-end becomes reconciliation archaeology instead of a governed opportunity-to-cash workflow.

04

No portfolio control tower

Delivery health, resource utilization, financial exposure, risk heatmaps, and approval bottlenecks never meet in one executive view. PMOs export Gantt snapshots; finance pulls P&L from spreadsheets; HR reports headcount separately. Leaders cannot drill from a portfolio KPI to the project, task, timesheet line, or approval that caused it — so decisions wait for weekly status meetings instead of live operational intelligence.

05

Approvals scattered across domains

Leave requests, timesheets, expenses, document sign-offs, asset assignments, project stage gates, budget changes, and invoice releases each run through a different approval path — or worse, email chains. Managers maintain mental queues across systems; delegation is informal; SLA breaches go untracked. Universal approval with business-impact context does not exist when every module owns its own inbox.

06

Integrations without observability

Salesforce, Jira, Entra ID, payroll providers, and HR systems sync in the background with no health dashboard, retry discipline, or trust scoring. When a connector fails, teams discover stale data days later — bid stages out of date, identities not provisioned, timesheet leave rows missing. IT cannot answer which external record maps to which internal entity without opening tickets and log files.

FAQ

Common questions

What is Tracopus?

Tracopus is a persona-aware Enterprise Work Intelligence Platform — twelve modules covering workspace, people (including ATS, LMS, and benefits), projects (including NPD and dossiers), resources, finance (billing through revenue recognition and vendor costs), payroll, performance, analytics, AI agents, integrations, admin, mobile, and client portal in one luxury operating layer with universal approvals and audit-first governance.

Who is Tracopus designed for?

Nine personas — employees, managers, project managers, resource managers, HR, payroll, finance, executives, and platform admins — across professional services, consulting, technology delivery, corporate operations, and sales-to-delivery organizations.

Can Tracopus support both project teams and HR teams?

Yes. Tracopus includes project delivery capabilities such as dashboards, projects, deliverables, tasks, task boards, capacity, files, reports, and collaboration. It also includes workforce capabilities such as employee records, profiles, timesheets, attendance, invoices, settings, and employee dashboards.

Does Tracopus support sales and contract workflows?

Yes. Sales teams can manage bid requests, bid details, opportunity stages, documents, purchase orders, contract records, and handoffs into delivery workflows.

Does Tracopus have a mobile experience?

Yes. The mobile experience supports project visibility, task updates, timesheet recording, and personal insights so users can stay productive away from the desktop.

Can managers view dashboards and reports?

Yes. Tracopus includes dashboards, charts, summaries, filters, and reports that help managers review project progress, workforce activity, utilization, capacity, sales progress, contract status, and operational trends based on their access level.
